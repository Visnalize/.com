# Four Rewarding Careers That Start With a Pre-Employment Medical

![Cover image](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1774931363306-5a896cd4dd02?auto=format&fit=crop&w=1280&q=80)

_Cover image by Hammilton Ojijo via [Unsplash](https://unsplash.com/photos/8eEkLj8HU_I)_

Choosing a course is a big step towards the kind of work you want to do. If you're drawn to hands-on, well-paid work, mining, rail, aviation and construction all deserve a spot on your shortlist.

Each of these careers also asks for a pre-employment medical before your first shift. Knowing that early means one less surprise when the job offer arrives.

Here's what each career offers, and what its medical involves.

## Careers Where Your Health Is Part of the Job

These are jobs where people rely on each other, so employers check that new starters are fit for the role. The medical also records your hearing, lungs and general health on day one.

That record stays useful for your whole career, giving every later check something to compare against.

### 1. Mining

Mining offers strong rewards for people starting out or switching careers. Jobs and Skills Australia puts the industry at 314,500 workers, with median weekly earnings of $2,832.

In NSW coal mining, the medical is written into law. Coal Services' Order 45 took effect on 1 January 2026, and in 2024-25 Coal Services recorded 10,836 pre-placement medicals for new entrants. If you're new to the industry, you're in good company.

Under the previous Order 43, pre-placement checks included a chest X-ray and respiratory fit testing, which makes sure a mask seals properly on your face.

### 2. Rail

Train driving is a skilled, well-paid role that's growing. Jobs and Skills Australia counts 18,700 train and tram drivers, with median weekly earnings of $3,001, and employment grew by 1,500 over the year.

Day to day, drivers watch signals, track conditions and speeds to keep passengers and freight moving safely.

Rail runs on one national standard, which makes the medical easy to plan for. Rail safety workers are assessed under the National Standard for Health Assessment of Rail Safety Workers, set by the National Transport Commission, and the current edition has applied since 11 November 2024. For safety-critical roles such as train driving, the doctor will check you're set up to stay alert and focused through a long shift.

### 3. Aviation

Aviation offers more than one way in, from the flight deck to the control tower. Jobs and Skills Australia counts 16,700 air transport professionals, with median weekly earnings of $2,651.

Your study choice can open the door here. Among air traffic controllers, 43.9% hold an advanced diploma or diploma and 34.6% hold a bachelor degree, so both vocational and university pathways lead into the role.

Pilots and air traffic controllers must hold a current medical certificate from the Civil Aviation Safety Authority (CASA). A commercial pilot licence needs a Class 1 certificate, usually valid for one year, and air traffic controllers need a Class 3. If flying is the dream, getting your Class 1 medical before you commit to commercial flight training lets you start with confidence.

### 4. Construction

Construction employs 1,371,500 people, with median weekly earnings of $1,600, according to Jobs and Skills Australia. The industry added 22,000 jobs over the past year, so there's room to grow whichever pathway you choose.

On sites where workers cut, grind or drill concrete, brick and stone, employers must provide health monitoring for workers exposed to respirable crystalline silica where there's a significant risk to their health. In NSW, those records are kept for 30 years, so your health history travels with you through your career.

## What Happens at a Pre-Employment Medical

Whichever career you choose, a [pre-employment medical assessment](https://www.assist-group.com.au/medicals/pre-employment-medicals) is matched to the demands of the job, so two people starting at the same company can have very different appointments. Depending on the role, yours may include:

- a health questionnaire covering your medical history and medications
- a hearing test in a sound booth
- a vision test
- a lung function test, where you blow hard into a tube
- a functional assessment of lifting, bending and reaching
- a drug and alcohol screen.

Bringing your glasses or hearing aids and a list of any medications helps the appointment run smoothly. Booking early leaves time for any follow-up tests before your start date.

## Can a Health Condition Rule You Out of a Career?

If you're managing a health condition, this is probably on your mind as you compare courses.

There's good news here. CASA notes that in most cases you can gain a medical certificate even when a medical issue is present. Where a condition needs managing, it may simply come with restrictions or extra monitoring.

It may be worth looking up the medical standard for your chosen career and talking it through with your GP before you enrol.

Then, when the job offer arrives, your pre-employment medical is one more step towards a career you're excited to start.
