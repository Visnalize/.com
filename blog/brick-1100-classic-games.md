![Cover image](/assets/covers/brick-1100-classic-games.jpg)

Most of them technically still exist somewhere, buried in an old phone, an offline browser tab, or an app store listing that hasn't been updated in a decade, so here they all are again in one place.

## 🐍 Snake

![Snake gameplay in Brick 1100](../brick1100/games/snake-gameplay.png)

Snake is the one that started it all. Programmed by Nokia's Taneli Armanto and [released on the Nokia 6110](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snake_(1998_video_game)) in 1998, it went on to ship on an estimated 350 million handsets, quietly teaching an entire generation the pain of clipping your own tail one pixel from a new high score.

Brick 1100's version keeps the same monochrome grid and the same one-button-too-many tension, plus a full campaign mode if the classic endless loop isn't enough.

## 🚀 Space Impact +

![Space Impact gameplay in Brick 1100](../brick1100/games/spaceimpact-gameplay.png)

Space Impact [debuted on the Nokia 3310](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Impact) in 2000 as a side-scrolling shooter, a genuine surprise on a phone whose main selling point was surviving a fall down the stairs. Over 150 million copies had shipped pre-installed by 2006, and CNET later named it one of the 10 greatest mobile games ever made.

Brick 1100's take, Space Impact +, keeps the original's left-to-right dodge-and-shoot gameplay and adds new levels, bonus weapons, and achievements on top.

👉 <DeepLink path="/games/space-impact" />

## 🧱 Brick Breaker

![Brick Breaker gameplay in Brick 1100](../brick1100/games/brick-gameplay.png)

[Brick Breaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brick_Breaker) traces back to Atari's Breakout, but most people of a certain age know it as the game built into BlackBerry devices since 1999, created by developer Ali Asaria to show off the color screens on early models. Bounce a ball off a paddle, clear the bricks, repeat.

Brick 1100's version adds extra levels and enhancements beyond the original formula, but the core paddle-and-ball loop is untouched.

## 🦖 Chrome Dino

![Chrome Dino gameplay in Brick 1100](../brick1100/games/dino-gameplay.png)

Officially the [Dinosaur Game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dinosaur_Game), built by a small Chrome team and released in September 2014, it pops up every time Wi-Fi drops mid-page-load. By the team's own count it was already being played around 270 million times a month by 2018, making it arguably the most-played browser game ever made, mostly by accident, since almost nobody opens it on purpose until the internet goes out.

Brick 1100 lets it be played on purpose for once, jump and duck included, with no need to unplug the router first.

👉 <DeepLink path="/games/dino" />

## 🧩 Monogram

![Monogram gameplay in Brick 1100](../brick1100/games/monogram-gameplay.png)

Monogram is a [nonogram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonogram) (also known as Picross or Griddlers) puzzle: fill or leave blank the cells of a grid based on number hints until a pixel image emerges. The format was invented independently by two people in Japan in 1987 and later popularized worldwide by Nintendo's Picross series, starting with Mario's Picross in 1995.

Monogram is the one game on this list with no direct Nokia-era equivalent, an original addition built for Brick 1100, fitting the phone's monochrome screen and keypad controls.

👉 <DeepLink path="/games/monogram" />

## ⚪ Rapid Roll

![Rapid Roll gameplay in Brick 1100](../brick1100/games/rapidroll-gameplay.png)

[Rapid Roll](https://www.igdb.com/games/rapid-roll) came a bit later, around 2005, pre-installed on a range of Nokia's budget and mid-range phones. The premise is simple and merciless: keep a ball rolling down a shaft of platforms and spikes, and the only difficulty setting is how steady your hands are.

It's every bit as simple, and every bit as addictive, in Brick 1100.

👉 <DeepLink path="/games/rapid-roll" />

## 🦆 Tappy Duck

![Tappy Duck gameplay in Brick 1100](../brick1100/games/tappyduck-gameplay.png)

Tappy Duck is Brick 1100's take on the Flappy Bird formula that took over every phone after its May 2013 release, racking up 90 million downloads before [creator Dong Nguyen pulled it from app stores](https://www.forbes.com/sites/lananhnguyen/2014/02/11/exclusive-flappy-bird-creator-dong-nguyen-says-app-gone-forever-because-it-was-an-addictive-product/) in February 2014, at the peak of its popularity. One button, one duck, an unreasonable number of pipes.

👉 <DeepLink path="/games/tappy-duck" />

## 📝 Wordee

![Wordee gameplay in Brick 1100](../brick1100/games/wordee-gameplay.png)

Wordee brings the [Wordle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wordle) formula, guess a hidden word in a handful of tries, to a T9 keypad. Wordle itself, created by Josh Wardle and released in October 2021, grew from about 90 players to hundreds of thousands within two months and was bought by The New York Times in January 2022. Wordee is Brick 1100's newest game, reworked for a phone that doesn't have a touchscreen keyboard.

## Common questions

### Do I need to install anything to play?

No. The [web version](https://brick1100.visnalize.com) runs every game listed above directly in a browser, with no login or download. The [Android/iOS app](https://get.brick1100.app/go) offers the same games plus the rest of the Nokia 1100 simulation (calls, SMS, reminders, and more) for offline use.

### Are these exact copies of the original games?

They're built from scratch to closely match the look, feel, and rules of the games they're inspired by, not technical ports or emulated code. See the difference explained in more detail in [Simulators vs. Emulators](./simulator-vs-emulator.md).

### Is it free?

Yes, all games are free to play, supported by ads. An optional one-time purchase or subscription removes ads and unlocks premium features.

### Can I see high scores or unlock achievements?

Yes, but game scores and achievements are only available in the [Android/iOS app](https://get.brick1100.app/go), not the web version.
