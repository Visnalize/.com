# Are You Overpaying for Web Hosting Every Single Month?

![Cover image](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1499951360447-b19be8fe80f5?auto=format&fit=crop&w=1280&q=80)

_Cover image by Domenico Loia via [Unsplash](https://unsplash.com/photos/macbook-pro-on-table-beside-white-imac-and-magic-mouse-hGV2TfOh0ns)_

Web hosting is one of those business expenses that might be forgotten after a few months. Payments might leave your account without much thought, your site stays online, and there might seem little reason to question it.

But hosting costs can change considerably depending on the plan, contract length, resources included, and services attached to the package. If your site has only modest traffic and fairly straightforward requirements, paying for resources you rarely use could mean wasted money.

## Check What Your Plan Includes

Hosting plans often bundle several features into a monthly price. Storage, bandwidth, email accounts, backups, security tools, website builders, domain registration, and technical support might all appear on the same invoice.

That doesn't mean you need every feature.

A small business site with a couple of dozen pages and five thousand monthly visitors doesn't require the same resources as an online store processing thousands of orders every day. If your hosting account provides 100GB of storage but your site uses 8GB, for example, the extra capacity might have little practical value.

Look through your current control panel and billing statement. Note the storage allocation, bandwidth limits, number of websites permitted, backup frequency, renewal price, and any paid add-ons. ## Check Renewal Prices

The price advertised when you first sign up isn't always the amount you'll pay long-term. Hosting companies often use introductory rates that apply for an initial billing period. The renewal price could be significantly higher once that period ends.

Suppose a hosting package costs $3 per month for the first year and renews at $8 per month. The initial $36 annual cost becomes $96 each year after renewal. Over three renewal years, that difference adds up to $180.

Check your next renewal date and the exact amount scheduled to be charged. If you signed up several years ago, the original promotional price may no longer be relevant.

## Match Hosting Resources to Your Site

Website requirements provide a good starting point for deciding whether your plan is oversized.

If you've got a personal blog, an [art portfolio](https://blog.milanartinstitute.com/how-to-build-an-art-portfolio/), or a brochure-style company site, you'll have different resource demands from a busy ecommerce store. Look at the traffic statistics and server usage, rather than choosing a plan based on the largest specs available.

Your site might often use only a fraction of its allocated storage and server resources, so moving to a smaller plan could reduce the monthly cost without changing the visitor experience.

A site that normally receives 5,000 visits per month but occasionally reaches 20,000 during seasonal campaigns may need more capacity at specific times. But that doesn't automatically justify paying for maximum resources throughout an entire year.

## Compare the Full Cost

Check whether the package includes [SSL certificates](https://www.cloudflare.com/learning/ssl/what-is-an-ssl-certificate/), backups, email hosting, migration assistance, and other services you currently pay for separately.

A three-year payment may produce a lower monthly equivalent while requiring a large upfront commitment. Monthly billing costs more in some cases but provides more flexibility, so it could be useful if you expect your requirements to change.

Before switching providers, calculate the annual cost of your current plan and compare it with equivalent packages elsewhere. Include renewal prices and any necessary extras.
